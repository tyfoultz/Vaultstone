Vaultstone · Character Sheet

Creative Layout Explorations

Starting from scratch — same character across all four, so you can compare apples to apples. Each option answers the core question differently: what should you never have to navigate to find?

Option A

The Atlas

Three permanent columns. Left: identity, HP, ability scores, saving throws — your character's foundation, always visible. Middle: tabbed content (just 3 tabs — Actions, Features, Journal). Right: the full skills list, always on screen. You never leave your character's stats to check a skill or make a saving throw. On mobile the right column becomes a dedicated Skills tab.

Option B

The Horizon

The top section is a visual "stage" — a gradient panel that holds the character portrait, name, HP (large, always editable), all 6 ability scores, and combat chips. It never scrolls away. Below it: just 3 tabs (Play, Reference, Lore) for deep content. On web, a narrow aside panel shows saving throws and conditions at all times alongside the content. Short/Long rest live in the stage. Feels premium. Ability scores are always in view.

Option C

The Scroll

No tabs at all. The character sheet is one long scrolling page — exactly like a real character sheet. A left-rail section navigator shows all sections and highlights where you are. Clicking a section name jumps to it. On mobile, a compact horizontal section bar replaces the left nav. Nothing is ever "hidden" behind a tab. The trade-off: more scrolling. Great for players who think spatially about their sheet.

Option D

The Cockpit

The main screen IS the combat dashboard — always. HP is huge and always editable. Attacks are listed. Spell slots are visible. Ability scores live in the identity panel. There are no tabs for these things. Skills, features, background, and notes live in a right-side drawer that slides in when you need it. On mobile the drawer becomes a bottom sheet. This is for the player who never wants to tap away from their combat info.
